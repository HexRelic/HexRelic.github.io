WitnessClip for Android, version 0.1.0

Recordings that prove themselves.

A voice recorder for conversations you take part in. It seals the audio while you record, so anyone can later check that nothing was changed and when it was made, without the app and without taking anyone's word for it.

Install it free Try to edit one

Free and open source. No account. Android 9 or newer, in English and Italian.

A normal audio file can be cut, spliced or re-dated with free software, and the result looks exactly like the original. WitnessClip records the proof together with the sound, so the first question about any recording, “has it been edited?”, has an answer.

Sealed while you record, not afterwards.

Here is the first minute of a recording, as WitnessClip writes it.

recording.flac0:00 recorded
0:000:100:200:300:400:501:00
certified timesigned

Every second

As soon as a second of audio is captured, it is hashed with SHA-256 and chained to the ones before it in a Merkle tree.

Every ten seconds

A key in the phone's secure hardware signs the chain. The key is made for this recording only, can't be copied out of the phone, and is destroyed once the recording is sealed.

At the start, every five minutes, at the stop

EU qualified timestamp services (Sectigo, ACCV and Izenpe) certify the time. Only a hash is sent, never the audio, and no account is needed.

Try to edit one.

This is a 30-second recording and its seal. Pick any second to change it, the way someone with an audio editor might. The check points to the exact second that no longer matches.

The real file is ordinary FLAC that plays in any player. The proof travels inside it.

recording.flac30 seconds, 48 kHz, 24-bit
30of 30 match
0:000:100:200:30
All 30 seconds match their seals. The recording is intact.

One button to start. The rest happens on its own.

Before you install.

Is my audio sent anywhere?

No. To get a certified time, the app sends only a hash of the audio to the timestamp services, never the audio itself. Those services are free and need no account. Your recordings stay on your phone until you export them.

Why can't I pause or trim a recording?

Because a gap or a cut is exactly what someone would question later. You can mark a moment or add a note instead, and interruptions are written into the seal rather than hidden.

What if my battery dies while recording?

The recording isn't lost. Everything up to the last second is recovered and sealed the next time the app starts, and marked as interrupted. The same happens if the system closes the app.

Will a court accept my recording?

That's for the court to decide. WitnessClip makes it possible to show that a file hasn't been changed and when it was made. What a recording proves explains what each check establishes and what it doesn't. The app doesn't give legal advice, and it's up to you to make sure recording is allowed where you are.

How do I know the app matches its source code?

Release builds are reproducible: building the same version from the public source gives a byte-identical app, so anyone can confirm the published app comes from that code.

What does it cost?

Nothing. WitnessClip is free and open source under the GNU GPL, version 3 or later, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

Was AI used to make it?

HexRelic develops the app with the help of large language models to speed up development, testing and fixes. The app itself contains no AI: recordings are never analysed, transcribed, enhanced or altered.
